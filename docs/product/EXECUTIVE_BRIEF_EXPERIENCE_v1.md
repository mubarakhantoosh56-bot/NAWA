# 1. Purpose

This document defines the executive experience of interacting with NAWA's Executive Decision Brief.

Its purpose is to ensure every executive receives information in the order their mind naturally processes decisions.

---

# 2. Executive Mindset

Executives rarely ask:

"What happened?"

They ask:

- Do I need to act?
- How serious is it?
- What is the business impact?
- What should I do next?
- Can I trust this recommendation?

The Executive Brief must answer these questions naturally.

---

# 3. Decision Journey

Every Executive Brief should guide the executive through five stages:

1. Awareness
2. Understanding
3. Evaluation
4. Decision
5. Follow-up

The executive should never feel lost during this journey.

---

# 4. Information Hierarchy

Information should always be presented in this order:

1. Executive Priority
2. Executive Summary
3. Executive Assessment
4. Business Impact
5. Executive Actions
6. Supporting Evidence
7. Remaining Uncertainty

The most important information must always appear first.

---

# 5. Reading Flow

The Executive Brief should be readable from top to bottom without requiring navigation between different screens.

Each section should naturally lead to the next.

---

# 6. Experience Principles

The experience should always feel:

- Clear
- Calm
- Professional
- Executive
- Trustworthy

The Executive Brief should reduce stress, not increase it.

---

# 7. Cognitive Rules

The Executive Brief should:

- Reduce cognitive load.
- Highlight what matters.
- Hide unnecessary complexity.
- Encourage confident decision making.
- Explain uncertainty honestly.

---

# 8. Success Criteria

A successful Executive Brief allows an executive to understand:

- What happened.
- Why it matters.
- Business impact.
- Recommended actions.
- Remaining uncertainty.

within approximately 60 seconds.

---

# 9. Never Do

The Executive Brief must never:

- Behave like an ERP report.
- Behave like a dashboard.
- Behave like a chat conversation.
- Force executives to search for important information.
- Prioritize technical details over business decisions.

---

# 10. Guiding Statement

The Executive Brief should feel like a trusted executive advisor sitting beside the decision maker.

Not a reporting system.
