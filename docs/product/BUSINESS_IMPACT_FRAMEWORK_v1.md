# 1. Purpose

This document defines how NAWA evaluates, prioritizes, and communicates business impact.

The purpose is not to measure operational events.

The purpose is to explain why those events matter to executive decision making.

---

# 2. Philosophy

Executives rarely make decisions because something happened.

Executives make decisions because something has business consequences.

Business Impact is the bridge between operational intelligence and executive decisions.

---

# 3. Impact Dimensions

Every Business Impact should be evaluated across one or more dimensions.

- Operational
- Financial
- Strategic
- Customer
- Compliance
- Reputation

Future domains may introduce additional dimensions.

---

# 4. Impact Levels

Every Business Impact should be classified as:

- Critical
- High
- Medium
- Low

Impact level must always be justified.

---

# 5. Assessment Rules

Business Impact should explain:

- What is affected?
- Why does it matter?
- What happens if no action is taken?
- Who is affected?
- How urgent is the impact?

---

# 6. Evidence Rules

Business Impact must never be based on assumptions.

If sufficient evidence is unavailable:

- State the uncertainty.
- Reduce confidence.
- Explain why.

---

# 7. Executive Language

Business Impact should always describe consequences using business language.

Avoid technical explanations whenever possible.

Executives should immediately understand why the situation deserves attention.

---

# 8. Future Expansion

This framework applies to every NAWA domain including:

- Poultry
- Manufacturing
- Sales
- Finance
- HR
- Warehousing
- CRM
- Distribution

---

# 9. Never Do

Business Impact must never:

- Exaggerate consequences.
- Hide uncertainty.
- Present unsupported financial claims.
- Mix recommendations with impact.
- Mix operational facts with business consequences.

---

# 10. Guiding Statement

Operational events become executive priorities only after their business impact is understood.
