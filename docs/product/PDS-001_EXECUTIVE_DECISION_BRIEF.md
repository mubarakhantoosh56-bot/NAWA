# 1. Purpose

Define the official product standard governing every Executive Decision Brief (EDB) generated by NAWA.

This document serves as the primary reference for Executive Decision Brief design, behavior, and structure.

---

# 2. Vision

The Executive Decision Brief is not a report.

It is the executive decision interface between NAWA and company leadership.

Its purpose is to improve executive decision quality through clear, evidence-based, business-oriented communication.

---

# 3. Scope

This standard applies to every Executive Decision Brief generated across all NAWA domains, including current and future industries.

### 5.1 Executive Priority

#### Purpose

Executive Priority is the first decision trigger in the Executive Decision Brief.

Its purpose is not to summarize the situation, but to answer one question before anything else:

> **Why should the executive care right now?**

The executive must understand the urgency, the business risk, and the required executive involvement within a few seconds.

---

#### Structure

Every Executive Priority contains four elements.

##### 1. Attention Level

Communicates the urgency of executive involvement.

Examples:

- Immediate Executive Attention Required
- Executive Review Recommended
- Executive Monitoring Recommended
- No Immediate Executive Action Required

##### 2. Situation

A one-sentence description of the operational condition requiring attention.

Example:

> Production performance has remained below operational expectations for three consecutive days.

##### 3. Business Risk

Explains the business consequence if the situation is ignored.

Example:

> Continued deterioration may affect operational planning and production stability.

##### 4. Executive Trigger

Clearly states what the executive should do next.

Example:

> Executive review is recommended before operational adjustments are approved.

---

#### Example

**Immediate Executive Attention Required**

Production performance has remained below operational expectations for three consecutive days.

Continued deterioration may affect operational planning and production stability.

Executive review is recommended before operational adjustments are approved.

---

#### Rules

- Maximum four short paragraphs.
- Readable in under ten seconds.
- Business language only.
- No technical implementation details.
- No operational metrics beyond what is necessary.
- Must drive executive attention, not simply describe operational data.

---

#### Success Criteria

After reading Executive Priority, the executive must immediately know:

- Is this important?
- Why is it important?
- What happens if it is ignored?
- What am I expected to do?

---

### 5.2 Executive Assessment

#### Purpose

Executive Assessment is the strategic interpretation of the current situation.

It does not repeat facts or operational observations.

Its role is to explain what the current evidence means from an executive perspective and why the situation deserves management attention.

This section represents NAWA's executive reasoning layer.

---

#### Structure

Every Executive Assessment contains three elements.

##### 1. Executive Interpretation

Explains what the available evidence indicates.

Example:

> Current evidence suggests that the production decline is no longer an isolated operational fluctuation.

##### 2. Business Meaning

Explains why the situation matters to management.

Example:

> The persistence of this trend indicates an operational condition that may require management intervention before productivity losses become systemic.

##### 3. Executive Recommendation Context

Provides executive context before presenting actions.

Example:

> Operational validation should be completed before management approves corrective actions.

---

#### Rules

- Never repeat facts already shown in previous sections.
- Never invent conclusions without supporting evidence.
- Explain meaning, not observations.
- Use executive business language.
- Remain objective and evidence-driven.
- Avoid operational implementation details.

---

#### Success Criteria

After reading Executive Assessment, the executive must understand:

- What does this situation actually mean?
- Why does it matter to management?
- Why is executive attention justified?

---

### 5.3 Business Impact

#### Purpose

Business Impact translates operational findings into executive business consequences.

Its purpose is to explain how the current situation may affect the organization from operational, financial, and strategic perspectives.

This section enables executives to prioritize decisions based on business impact rather than operational observations.

---

#### Structure

Every Business Impact contains three dimensions.

##### 1. Operational Impact

Explains the expected effect on day-to-day operations.

Example:

> Continued production decline may reduce operational efficiency and require adjustments to production planning.

##### 2. Financial Impact

Explains the expected financial consequence.

If sufficient financial evidence is unavailable, NAWA must explicitly state that the financial impact cannot yet be determined.

Example:

> Financial impact cannot currently be quantified because the required production cost and revenue inputs are unavailable.

##### 3. Strategic Impact

Explains the potential long-term consequence if the situation continues.

Example:

> If the current trend continues, management planning assumptions and future production targets may require revision.

---

#### Rules

- Always separate Operational, Financial, and Strategic impacts.
- Never estimate financial losses without evidence.
- Never exaggerate business consequences.
- Clearly distinguish confirmed impacts from potential impacts.
- Every impact statement must be supported by available evidence.
- When evidence is insufficient, explicitly acknowledge the limitation.

---

#### Success Criteria

After reading Business Impact, the executive must understand:

- How operations may be affected.
- Whether there is a measurable financial consequence.
- Whether the situation may influence future business decisions.

---

### 5.4 Executive Actions

#### Purpose

Executive Actions translate executive understanding into executive decisions.

Their purpose is not to describe operational tasks, but to recommend management-level actions that support informed decision-making.

Every action should help the executive decide what should happen next, why it should happen, and what outcome is expected.

---

#### Structure

Every Executive Action contains five elements.

##### 1. Recommended Action

A concise management-level action.

Example:

> Validate feed performance before approving production adjustments.

##### 2. Business Rationale

Explains why this action is recommended.

Example:

> Current evidence is insufficient to conclude that feed quality is not contributing to the production decline.

##### 3. Expected Outcome

Describes the intended business result if the action is completed.

Example:

> Reduces decision uncertainty and enables corrective actions to be based on verified operational evidence.

##### 4. Priority

Defines the urgency of the action.

Possible values:

- Critical
- High
- Medium
- Low

##### 5. Owner

Identifies the management role responsible for ensuring the action is completed.

Examples:

- CEO
- COO
- Operations Manager
- Farm Manager
- Finance Manager

If the accountable role cannot be determined from available company context, NAWA must state:

> Accountable role not yet assigned.

NAWA must never invent ownership.

---

#### Rules

- Recommend executive decisions, not operational procedures.
- Every action must have a clear business purpose.
- Every action must produce a measurable expected outcome.
- Never recommend actions that are unsupported by available evidence.
- Never request Company Inputs in this section.
- Company Inputs belong exclusively to the Recommended Company Inputs section.

---

#### Success Criteria

After reading Executive Actions, the executive must understand:

- What decision is recommended.
- Why this decision is recommended.
- What business outcome is expected.
- Who is responsible for driving the action.
- Which actions should be prioritized first.

# 6. Product Principles

The Executive Decision Brief follows these principles:

- Executive First
- Decision Before Data
- Truth Before Reasoning
- Evidence Before Confidence
- Business Before Technical
- Action Oriented
- Confidence Transparency
- 60-Second Rule

Detailed definitions are maintained inside the supporting standards.

# 8. Success Criteria

An Executive Decision Brief is successful when an executive can understand:

- What happened.
- Why it matters.
- Business impact.
- Recommended actions.
- Remaining uncertainty.

within approximately 60 seconds.
