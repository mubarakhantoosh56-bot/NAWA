# 1. Purpose

This document defines the product philosophy and design principles governing every Executive Decision Brief (EDB) generated by NAWA.

It establishes how NAWA communicates with executives, ensuring every brief is designed to improve decision quality rather than simply present operational information.

These principles apply across all current and future NAWA domains.

---

# 2. Vision

The Executive Decision Brief is not a report.

It is the executive decision interface between NAWA and company leadership.

Its purpose is to reduce uncertainty, improve decision quality, and help executives understand what matters most, why it matters, and what should happen next.

---

# 3. Design Philosophy

NAWA does not compete with ERP systems.

ERP systems record operations.

NAWA explains operations.

The Executive Brief exists to transform operational intelligence into executive decisions.

---

# 4. Core Principles

## 4.1 Executive First

Every Executive Brief is written for decision makers.

The first screen should answer the executive's most important questions before presenting supporting information.

---

## 4.2 Decision Before Data

Raw operational data is never the goal.

Every section must support a business decision.

---

## 4.3 Truth Before Reasoning

Verified facts always appear before observations, interpretations, or recommendations.

Facts are never mixed with opinions.

---

## 4.4 Evidence Before Confidence

Every confidence level must be supported by available evidence.

Missing evidence must always be disclosed.

Confidence must never hide uncertainty.

---

## 4.5 Business Before Technical

Executives think in business outcomes.

The Executive Brief should use business language instead of technical terminology whenever possible.

---

## 4.6 Action Oriented

Every Executive Brief should help management decide what to do next.

Information without action has limited executive value.

---

## 4.7 Business Impact First

Every operational finding must explain its expected business consequence.

Executives prioritize impact, not events.

---

## 4.8 Confidence Transparency

Confidence is not a score.

Confidence is an explanation.

Every confidence level should clearly explain:

- why confidence exists,
- what evidence supports it,
- what evidence is still missing.

---

## 4.9 Simplicity

Complexity belongs inside the AI.

Simplicity belongs inside the Executive Brief.

---

## 4.10 Cognitive Load Reduction

Executives operate under time pressure.

The Executive Brief should minimize unnecessary reading and maximize decision clarity.

---

## 4.11 60-Second Rule

An executive should understand:

- what happened,
- why it matters,
- business impact,
- recommended actions,
- remaining uncertainty

within approximately one minute.

---

# 5. Never Do

The Executive Brief must never:

- present unsupported statements.
- hide uncertainty.
- mix facts with assumptions.
- overload executives with unnecessary operational details.
- recommend actions without evidence.
- expose internal AI reasoning.
- expose technical implementation details.
- behave like a dashboard or ERP report.

---

# 6. Product Standard

These principles apply to every Executive Decision Brief generated by NAWA regardless of industry.

Including, but not limited to:

- Poultry
- Manufacturing
- Sales
- Finance
- Warehousing
- HR
- CRM
- Distribution
- Healthcare
- Future AI Domains

---

# 7. Future Evolution

These principles represent Version 1 of the Executive Decision Brief philosophy.

Future versions may extend these principles while preserving their core purpose:

Helping executives make better decisions.

---

# 8. Guiding Statement

Executives do not buy reports.

Executives buy better decisions.

The Executive Decision Brief exists to improve executive decision quality.
